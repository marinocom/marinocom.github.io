researchpublication

          

            
HistoCrypt 2026 · 9th International Conference on Historical Cryptology

            
Joint Transcription and Decryption of Images of Encrypted Handwritten Documents: A Comparison with the Traditional Pipeline

            
Marino Oliveros-Blanco, Lei Kang, Alicia Fornés, Beáta Megyesi
Computer Vision Center (UAB) · Stockholm University

            
Deciphering historical encrypted manuscripts usually takes two steps: transcribe the cipher symbols from the image, then decrypt them. Transcription errors propagate straight into the plaintext. We propose Direct Image Decryption, an end-to-end model that maps manuscript images straight to plaintext. Using the Copiale cipher as a case study, we built a synthetic data pipeline for large-scale training and compared the joint model against the traditional pipeline. Joint image-to-plaintext modelling turns out to be a promising alternative.

about

          
Some of the most interesting writing in the world still can't be read. Rongorongo, the glyphs carved into a couple of dozen wooden tablets from Rapa Nui, has never been deciphered. The Inca kept their records in khipu, knotted cords whose numbers we have mostly worked out but whose stories we still can't read.

          
That's the work I want to do: recover what was written, recorded or encoded and then lost. My research so far taught a model to read the Copiale cipher, an 18th-century secret society's manuscript, straight from the page. Next I want to take the same ideas to scripts no one has cracked, and to lost media.

          
I hold a BSc in Artificial Intelligence from UAB, with a year at Kyoto University. Away from research: films, the hunt for cheap coffee, odd little music tools, and debate.
